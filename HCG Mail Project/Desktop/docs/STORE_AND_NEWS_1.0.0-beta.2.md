## HCG Store — Windows edition

**Title:** HCG Mail

**Subtitle:** Secure email for your own mailbox — now steadier on school and public Wi-Fi

**Release:** 1.0.0-beta.2 (build 4) · Public beta · Free during testing · Windows 10/11, x64 and ARM64

### What's new in this beta

- **Network Doctor.** When mail stops on a school, hotel or guest network, Mail Health › Network Doctor checks the
  connection step by step:
  - your connection and Wi-Fi sign-in pages;
  - whether the network lets email through;
  - your mail servers, their certificates and your sign-in.

  It then says whether the network, your password, the server or the app is most likely the cause. It also says how
  sure it is: confirmed, likely, or undetermined.
- **Reconnects by itself.** After the network drops, changes, or your PC wakes up, HCG Mail reconnects automatically.
  It waits longer between attempts, up to 5 minutes, and tells you what it is doing. A wrong password is never tried
  again on its own.
- **Read mail offline.** Messages you have read stay readable without a connection. The copy on your PC is encrypted,
  with a separate key per account kept in Windows Credential Manager.
- **Outbox.** Messages written while offline wait in the Outbox and are sent when the connection is back. A message that
  may already have gone out is never sent twice without asking you.
- **Another way to send.** If a network blocks your mail provider's sending port, Network Doctor can offer another port
  your provider documents, still fully encrypted.
- **Cleaner look.** The official HCG logo without a background box, one HCG Mail title (no overlapping text), and a
  simpler welcome screen.

### Privacy

- Offline copies and the Outbox stay on your PC, encrypted. They are never uploaded to HCG or anyone else.
- In Settings › Storage you can choose how long copies are kept and how much space they may use, or turn them off and
  delete them.
- Network Doctor contacts a public connectivity service only while you run it. You choose Google, Apple or Microsoft, or
  turn these checks off, in Settings › Privacy & security.

### Good to know

- **Accounts:** IMAP and SMTP accounts (for example Domene.no, Gmail, iCloud, Yahoo or Fastmail with an app password).
- **Upgrading:** installing over 1.0.0-beta.1 keeps your accounts, settings and saved passwords.
- **Not code-signed yet:** Windows SmartScreen shows a warning. Check the SHA-256 checksum on this page before running.
- **Display scaling:** tested at 100–175 %; **200 % has not been tested yet**.
- **Backups:** removing an account deletes its offline copy and keys from your PC. Backups of your PC made earlier may
  still contain older encrypted copies.

## HCG News — announcement

**Headline:** HCG Mail beta 2: built for school and public Wi-Fi

**Teaser:** The second public beta of HCG Mail finds out why mail stops on a restricted network, reconnects by itself,
and keeps your mail readable offline — without ever loosening security.

**Body:**

School, hotel and guest networks often get in the way of email. Some close the ports mail uses. Some show a sign-in page
first. Some inspect encrypted traffic. Until now, mail just stopped. In HCG Mail 1.0.0-beta.2 for Windows it no longer
stops quietly.

The new **Network Doctor** in Mail Health checks your connection step by step and tells you, in plain words, whether the
network, your password, your mail provider or the app is the likely cause. It also says how sure it is. When a network
blocks your provider's usual sending port, it can suggest another port the provider documents. The connection stays
fully encrypted.

HCG Mail now **reconnects by itself** when the network comes back, when you switch networks, or when your computer wakes
up. It waits a little longer after each attempt so it never floods the network, and it never retries a wrong password on
its own.

Mail you have read **stays readable offline**, and messages you write offline wait in a new **Outbox** until they can
be sent. If a connection breaks while a message is being sent, HCG Mail asks you before sending it again rather than
risk sending it twice.

Your offline mail is encrypted on your computer with a separate key for each account, kept in the system's credential
store, and it is never uploaded to HCG. You decide how long it is kept, and you can delete it at any time. Certificate
checks and encryption stay on, on every network.

HCG Mail 1.0.0-beta.2 is free during the public beta and available for Windows (x64 and ARM64). If you installed
beta 1, install the new version over it: your accounts, settings and passwords are kept. The macOS edition follows once
it has been signed and notarized.

**Known limitations:**
- The Windows build is not code-signed yet, so SmartScreen warns.
- 200 % display scaling has not been tested.
